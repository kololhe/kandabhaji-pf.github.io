Abstract

In real-time autonomous field applications, GNSS is often unreliable and inaccurate. A possible solution is localizing against a prebuilt map like a satellite image, as it is georeferenced and already surveyed. In this work, we localize a spinning radar measurement against overhead RGB imagery with a learned observation model. We use a radar representation pretrained by masked reconstruction and align it to satellite-pretrained features. A particle filter propagated by radar odometry proposes candidate poses, and our observation model provides a likelihood over these poses. We evaluate our method over 31 distinct routes, over 8 datasets spanning both urban and offroad conditions using only ground radar and a coarse local prior. We also show the robustness over changing weather using repeated traversals over the same route over 13 months, in both urban and offroad conditions.

Results

Ground truth in red, the particle-filter estimate in green, and the scan-matching odometry the filter propagates on as a dashed blue line. The odometry wanders hundreds of metres on every route; the estimate stays on ground truth. None of these routes, and no map covering them, appears anywhere in training.

What the filter is doing

The per-frame error says how far the estimate is wrong, not what shape the posterior has. Here the particle cloud is drawn on the imagery it is being scored against, so the failure modes become legible: an along-track ambiguity is a cloud smeared down the road, and a loss of lock is a cloud that contracts around the wrong mode.
