Abstract

Localising a ground vehicle against public overhead imagery is attractive because the map is free, global and already surveyed, but the sensor and the map are captured years apart, in different seasons, by different instruments. We match scanning radar to satellite imagery in a shared learnt representation: a radar encoder pretrained by masked reconstruction is aligned by a contrastive objective to frozen self-supervised features of the orthophoto on a common 4.8 m metric grid, with a distinctiveness head that down-weights cells whose content the sweep does not determine. Because the model ranks candidate poses rather than regressing one, the estimator is a particle filter. We hold out whole datasets rather than splitting them geographically, so no sequence, route or map from an evaluated dataset is seen in training.

Results

Ground truth in red, the particle-filter estimate in green, and the scan-matching odometry the filter propagates on as a dashed blue line. The odometry wanders hundreds of metres on every route; the estimate stays on ground truth. None of these routes, and no map covering them, appears anywhere in training.

What the filter is doing

The per-frame error says how far the estimate is wrong, not what shape the posterior has. Here the particle cloud is drawn on the imagery it is being scored against, so the failure modes become legible: an along-track ambiguity is a cloud smeared down the road, and a loss of lock is a cloud that contracts around the wrong mode.
